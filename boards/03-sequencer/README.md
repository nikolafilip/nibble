# Board 03: sequencer

The control unit: step counter, instruction and operand registers, flags, two
decoders and the diode control matrix. 524 transistors, 298 resistors, 83
matrix diodes, 19 LEDs. The program counter and the return register are on
board 09 (D035).

| | |
|---|---|
| Bus signals used | in: M0..M7, CF, ZF, CLK, RST. out: II, PCE, SUB, EO, AI, AO, BI, BO, BA, AB, OI, IO, PCL, FI, HLT, MAI, MI, MO, ONE, F0, F1, INP |
| Bus driven | BUS0#..BUS3# open-drain with OPR3..0 while IO (LDI, LOAD n, STORE n) |
| Link header (2x6) | to board 09: OPR0..OPR7, PCR, RAI, GND, GND |

How it runs an instruction (docs/isa.md):

- **Steps** are a one-hot ring of five flip-flops with asynchronous reset. S0
  loads IR and the operand's low nibble (`II`); S1 counts (`PCE`); S2, only for
  a two-word instruction, loads the second word (`OPI`, `PCE`); S3 and S4 drive
  the matrix rows. The `DONE` column returns the ring to S0.
- **IR and OPR** are transparent latches open while their load line and PH1
  are both high: they follow the memory word during the low half of the clock
  and are closed before the rising edge on which everything else changes.
- **Decoders**: IR3..0 to OP0#..OP15#, OPR3..0 to F0#..F15#, each through a
  pair of 2-to-4 predecoders so no NAND has more than two transistors in series.
- **Rows**: `NOR(OPn#, T3N)` for every opcode, `NOR(OP0#, Fn#, T3N)` for every
  family member, an S4 row for the four memory accesses, and for JC and JZ a
  second row that includes the latched flag. 37 rows, 10k pull-ups.
- **The matrix**: a diode from a row to a column pulls that control line high
  while the row is active. 23 columns with 1 Meg pull-downs: 19 go to the bus
  header, `PCL` too, `PCR` and `RAI` to the link, `DONE` back to the step ring.
  New instructions are diodes; rows OP8..OP15 are free.
- **Flags**: CFQ and ZFQ latched from the ALU while `FI` and PH1.

## Files

- `sequencer.kicad_sch`: generated by `sim/build_sequencer.py` from `sim/sequencer.py`. Do not hand-edit.
- `sequencer.kicad_pcb`, `fab/`: layout by `sim/pcb.py`. The matrix is laid out transposed: rows are vertical tracks at 3.81 mm pitch on the back, columns horizontal tracks at 10.16 mm on the front, a diode standing on each populated crossing.

## Verification

The machine gate (`sim/gate.py`): the reference programs on the kicad-cli
exports of ALU, registers, sequencer and counter with the hub, compared tick
for tick with `sim/emu.py`. Results are recorded in the commit that adds the
routed board.

D041 proof (2026-09-11): list.asm on alu, hub, registers, sequencer, counter and memory at the MIX corner, seed 3, the run that failed before the column buffers: 210 ticks, 0 mismatches (`sim/out/d041_list_MIX3.log`). Seeds 1 and 2 and the other programs are in the card gate.

Layout (D051, 2026-09-11, amended 2026-09-13): 245 x 255 mm on four layers, the tiles at the card pitch; layer 2 is a ground pour with a few routed tracks, layer 3 a third routing layer (two layers left the router about 100 nets short at this pitch and about 70 at 10.16 mm, D049). Routed with freerouting 2.4.1 (Java 25) with the layers ordered so that layers 3 and 4 route vertically and 1 and 2 horizontally, the power nets out of its network (the rails, stubs and vias are fixed copper) and the optimizer off. The matrix rows and columns are hidden from the router on the outer layers and a keepout fences the matrix, but the matrix diodes stay in the DSN with their pins off the nets, so layer 3 threads between their pads across it. Each matrix column tail ends on a fixed via 4.5 mm before the keepout with a short stub on layer 3, where the router picks it up on its vertical layer. The 45 row drivers stand under their matrix columns (three per tile column, left to right). The left field, 12 columns, is split: its right five columns start one tile row lower, leaving an 8 mm strip under the header's right half for the fan-out to pins 42 to 64, and the output buffers stand in the column under their own header pin; the 23 column buffers fill the columns beside the matrix, where the tails come out. The +5V feed runs along the top edge and down the right side of the board, never through the corridor between the left field and the matrix.

Routed board (2026-09-14, route v9): 0 unconnected, 0 DRC errors on the rules it was routed with (0.2 mm track, 0.15 mm clearance). The warnings are by design: 59 dangling fixed stubs (the 45 matrix column tails and the In2 stubs of the row tails the router did not need) and silkscreen overlaps of the two decoupling capacitors' references. One hand patch after the router, recorded in the plan (`extra.patches`) and applied by `pcb.py`: freerouting had hooked ENF across CFQ_m1 on layer 3 beside Q380. The SPICE export of this schematic is line for line the one the machine gate ran (resistor pin order aside): the tile mirroring of the re-placement is the only difference. Gate results on the card deck with this sequencer: all twelve programs at TYP, 0 mismatches (lfsr 1497 ticks, rerun with the virtual reset fixed in machine.py, `sim/out/cards_lfsr_TYP.log`); fib, alu, gcd, list, logic, call at LO, HI, MIX1 and MIX2: 24 of 24 runs, 0 mismatches (`sim/out/gate_cards_corners.log`); fib, list, logic, calc at TYP and LO with 500 pF per bus line: 8 of 8; fib and call with the clock card driving CLK: 0 mismatches (docs/cards.md step 9).
