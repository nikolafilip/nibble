The four faces, to scale

Each face is 460 mm between the uprights. A board sits on two horizontal rails, one under its top holes and one under its bottom holes, so a board of any width mounts anywhere on a face. The left face is empty until you need a third program page.

How a board mounts

Cross-section through one rail, looking along it.

  1. Frame. Four 900 mm uprights of 2020 extrusion at the corners of a 500 mm square, joined top and bottom by 460 mm rails with corner brackets. Rubber feet or a 500 mm plywood square underneath.
  2. Rails. Two 460 mm rails per board, clamped to the uprights with corner brackets at the height of the board's top and bottom hole rows. The 3D model has them at the heights in the table below.
  3. Boards. An M3 T-nut slides into the rail slot at each hole. A 12 mm M3 standoff screws into the T-nut; the board screws onto the standoff. Nothing is drilled, so a board moves with an Allen key.
  4. Hub. Two rails at 450 mm on the left and right faces carry two cross rails 107 mm apart, the hub's hole pitch. The hub lies flat on them on standoffs, headers up, USB socket toward the back, so every ribbon drops onto it from above and its power LED is visible from the top.
  5. Cables. Every board's 64-way header is a shrouded IDC socket 8 mm from its top edge (the panel's is on its bottom edge). The ribbon leaves it straight out of the face, folds 90° up over the board's top edge, and runs down inside to the hub. Two folds and one slack loop per cable, no tight angles, the way a floppy cable was routed. A second and later program page hangs off the page before it: the program board has two 64-way headers wired in parallel, hub in on the left, next page out on the right, so pages side by side are joined by a 10 cm loop.
  6. Power. One USB cable out of the bottom of the column to a phone charger.

Rail heights and ribbon lengths

Rails measured from the floor to the rail centre. Ribbon length is the path from the board's header, out and over its edge, inside to the hub at 450 mm, plus 100 mm of slack. Boards are centred on their face; the counter and the sequencer share the front; the two program pages sit side by side. A program page after the first has no cable to the hub: its ribbon hops from the second header of the page before it (D039), and the length shown is that hop.

Stock 64-way IDC cables come in 30, 50 and 100 cm; or 3 m of 64-way ribbon and 16 IDC sockets, crimped in a bench vice, make every cable the exact length. Eight cables of half a metre add about 0.3 nF to each bus line; with the hub's 10k pull-ups that is 3 µs of rise time on a 1 ms tick, and the machine deck now includes it.

The extrusion is the same 2020 profile that 3D printers use, so every part here is a stock Amazon item and arrives in two days. It also means a sixth program page or a whole new board is two rails and four T-nuts, no new frame.

What it does not do

The ribbons show: each one leaves its socket toward you, folds up over the board's edge and disappears inside. They are the visible wiring of the machine. It does not put the panel at standing height: on the floor the switches are at 65 to 90 cm, on a 40 cm table they are at chest height. And the column has a back: the two program pages and the ALU face away from wherever you put it, so it wants a spot you can walk around, not a corner.
