The two faces, to scale

Both faces are five columns of cards at a 110 mm pitch, 20 mm inside the uprights' outer edges. A card's two holes are mid-height on its sides, so one rail per row carries the whole row; the front has four rows above a band of three where the sequencer (245 × 255, four corner holes 231 mm apart) stands on two rails of its own with the clock, the panel cards and the hub beside it on the same rails. The bus ribbons run behind the cards (grey, seen in the gaps), down one column and up the next; the 12-way chains run over the fronts of the counter columns and the memory column (sand), and the 6-way carry links round the column gaps (thin lines).

How a card mounts

Cross-section through one rail, looking along it: the card hangs on two standoffs, one at each side hole; the bus ribbon passes between card and rail.

  1. Frame. Two 800 mm uprights of 2020 extrusion, 580 mm apart outside to outside, each standing on a 300 mm foot of the same extrusion laid flat across the panel (two corner brackets). Nothing is drilled.
  2. Rails. A 580 mm rail across the uprights' front faces for every row of the front, and across their back faces for every row of the back, at the height of the row's holes, one corner bracket at each end. Fourteen rails. The front's fifth and seventh rails are the sequencer's.
  3. Cards. An M3 T-nut slides into the rail's front slot at each of the card's two side holes. A 12 mm M3 standoff screws into the T-nut; the card screws onto the standoff. A card moves with two T-nuts. The standoff must clear the socket on the card's header; that is measured on the pilot's hub before the standoffs are bought.
  4. Hub. Bottom right of the front, a header along its top edge (ribbon A, up the front) and one along its bottom edge (ribbon B, under the panel and up the back), both on its back; its two banana sockets on its front, about 9 cm above the table.
  5. Ribbons. Every card's 64-way header is on its back along its top edge. The ribbon runs down a column behind the cards, between the standoffs, with a socket crimped on wherever there is a card, and turns at the top or bottom of a column with three folds (docs/mounting.md).
  6. Power. A bench supply at 5.0 V with its limit at 1.5 A, two banana leads into the hub's sockets. Nothing else powers the machine.

Rails and ribbons

Rail heights are from the table top to the rail's centre (the row's holes). A ribbon's length is its route plus 40 mm for every turn and 300 mm of slack.

The extrusion is the 2020 profile that 3D printers use, so every part is a stock item cut to length. A row without cards needs no rail, and a longer program is more cards on the same ribbon, up the back's last column.

What it does not do

It has a back: the memory and the program are behind what you stand in front of, so it wants a table you can walk round, or it turns on its feet. The switches and buttons of the panel cards are 4 to 26 cm above the table top, seated height. The 12-way chains lie over the fronts of the counter and memory cards, across the transistors where no LED stands: visible wiring, by design.
